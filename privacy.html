Privacy Policy

Version 1.3 · Last updated 2 November 2026

This policy explains what happens to your data when you play Conjunction (the “app”), an Android game distributed through Google Play, and when you visit this website. It covers Conjunction 0.6.2 and later, which offer two optional online features: cloud save and gameplay statistics. Earlier versions offer neither and send none of the data described under Cloud save and Usage statistics. What changed since version 1.2 is listed under Changes to this policy.

The short version

Your saves stay on your device. Two features can send data, each only if you tick it, and the game plays the same without either: a backup of your heroes in the cloud, and gameplay statistics, for which we ask your consent and which are only for players aged 16 or older. Both boxes start empty, and you can change either at any time under Settings → Gameplay → Privacy. There is no advertising and no tracking across other apps. A feedback report reaches us only when you send it yourself.

What the app keeps on your device

The app keeps these on your device, in its private storage, and sends none of them unless you choose one of the features below or send a feedback report:

  • Your saves — each hero’s character, name as you chose it, progress, inventory, claimed fields and settings, so you can go on playing. They are needed to provide the game you play.
  • Your privacy choices — the two boxes, the version of the notice you answered, and whether you said you are 16 or older, so the app can respect your choices and does not ask again. No birth date is asked or kept.
  • Fight recordings — the last fights, including any note you add with “Felt wrong?”, so a fight can be traced if you report a problem. They leave the device only inside a feedback report you send.
  • The play diary — only while you share gameplay statistics: one line for each moment of play (the app was started or brought back, a fight began or ended, a field was cleared for the first time, the hero died, a field’s entry check turned the hero away, the hero switched equipment set, a hunt was collected, a level was reached). A line holds game values such as the field, the hero’s level, the outcome, how long it took and what was found, a random identifier for the hero and one for the current app session, the app and game-data version, whether the game’s test options were used, and the time. While you also use cloud save, a line holds your cloud Player ID as well, so the statistics of your phones can be linked. It holds no name and nothing you typed. Unticking the statistics deletes the diary of every hero on the device.

Removing the app normally removes its private storage. A feedback archive you saved to Downloads, and any copy in a device backup, follow the settings of that storage. Neither removing the app nor unticking a box deletes what was already sent; see Your rights.

Before anything is sent

The first time you start the app — or, if you played an earlier version, the next time you open the world map — it shows a notice in two steps. The first names who is responsible and both features and asks once whether you are 16 or older. The second names in short what each feature sends and to whom, with a box for each, both empty, and points to this policy for the details. Below 16, the statistics box is not offered here; cloud save stays available. If you turn 16 later, you can correct your answer in Settings and then decide on the statistics. If you continue without ticking, you play without either feature.

Before you choose an online feature, your saves, settings and privacy choices remain on your device. The app does not start the statistics service or keep the play diary before you agree, and it does not create a cloud account before you tick cloud save. Cloud save uses only the identifiers and session data needed for the backup you asked for. Unticking the statistics stops new statistics and removes the diary; the app may still contact Unity once to ask for the statistics to be erased, and cloud save goes on if you ticked it. When you tick a box later in Settings, the app first shows what that feature sends and asks you to confirm.

Cloud save

Cloud save backs up each hero’s whole save — the hero’s name as you chose it, level, equipment, bag, gold, story progress and the hero’s random identifier — to Unity Gaming Services Cloud Save. The copy is sent about twenty seconds after the game saves, and only while cloud save is ticked; unticking it stops new copies, although a copy already on its way may still arrive.

To keep the copies apart, Unity Gaming Services Authentication gives the app a random Player ID when cloud save first connects, and keeps a session on your device so the app can reconnect without asking you. The device stores that Player ID and the session data. Settings shows your Player ID.

To restore your heroes on another phone, first link Google sign-in (Google Play Games) in Settings, then use the same sign-in on the new phone. Google then authenticates you and gives the app a one-time code, which Unity exchanges for your Google Play Games account identifier and links to your Player ID. A version of the app that does not offer Google sign-in says so in Settings; there the backup serves only this phone. Google Ireland Limited also processes your sign-in under its own privacy policy, as an independent controller. The sign-in is never required.

Legal basis: providing the backup and sign-in you request by ticking cloud save and linking Google, Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR; storing the Player ID and session on your device is strictly necessary for that service (§ 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG). The copies, the account and the Google link are kept until you delete the cloud account, in the app under Settings → Gameplay → Account or by writing to us. Unticking cloud save alone does not delete them.

Usage statistics

If you are 16 or older and agree, the app sends the lines of the play diary to Unity Gaming Services Analytics so that we can see where the game is too hard or unclear and balance it. The game defines nine moments of play: app started or brought back, fight started, fight ended, field cleared for the first time, hero died, entry check failed, equipment set switched, hunt collected and level reached. Unity Analytics also records its own standard events, such as when a play session starts and ends.

Analytics gives the app a random installation identifier, stored on your device; if you also use cloud save, your Player ID is attached as well, so the statistics of a cloud player can be linked to that Player ID. With the random hero and session identifiers in the diary, this makes the statistics pseudonymous: we do not use them to identify you, do not combine them with other data and do not use them for advertising. Analytics also processes your IP address, the approximate region derived from it, and technical diagnostics and performance data of the app. The diary holds no name; a hero’s name reaches Unity only inside a cloud save copy.

Unity documents that it processes Analytics data on servers in Belgium and the Netherlands and keeps a replicated copy in Iowa, United States. Raw events are kept for 13 months; the counts and player profiles derived from them are kept until deletion is requested.

Legal basis: your consent, given by ticking I agree to gameplay statistics, Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, which also covers the diary, the identifiers and the events the app keeps on your device for this purpose (§ 25(1) TDDDG). You can withdraw your consent at any time by unticking the statistics under Settings → Gameplay → Privacy; this is as easy as giving it and does not affect the lawfulness of the processing before you withdrew. From then on the app collects no new statistics, deletes the diary on your device, and asks Unity to delete what it received from this installation — if your device is offline, at the next start that reaches Unity. Unity deletes the data within seven days of receiving the request.

Feedback reports

The game has a feedback button for reporting bugs and ideas. When you tap it and send, the app packs one file containing:

  • the text you typed and whether it is a bug or an idea;
  • the app version, your device model and its Android version;
  • the save game of every hero on this device — each character and the name you gave it, progress, inventory, skills and settings, and whether the game’s test options were used;
  • the play diary of every hero on this device, if you share gameplay statistics;
  • the fight recordings not sent in an earlier report, including any note you added to a fight with “Felt wrong?”.

The app does not send this file itself. It saves the file in your device’s Downloads folder and opens Android’s share menu. You choose the app it goes through (for example your e-mail app or Discord) and the recipient; for e-mail the address feedback@conjunctiongame.com is filled in for you. If you cancel, nothing is sent. The copy in Downloads stays until your next report replaces it or you delete it.

We use what you send only to find and fix errors and to balance the game. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in doing that, Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR. We keep a report for as long as we are working on what it describes, and delete it earlier if you ask. The app you send it through also handles the report, along with whatever it carries with it, such as your e-mail address or Discord name, under its own terms. If you post a report in a channel that other people can read, they can see it too.

Permissions the app requests

  • Network access — for the cloud save and the gameplay statistics while you have ticked them, for the optional Google sign-in, and to ask Unity to erase statistics after you untick them.
  • Vibration — for haptic feedback on critical hits. No data is read or transmitted.
  • Notifications — so the game can tell you when a field you set to farm has finished. These notifications are generated on your device by the app itself; no server is involved and no information is sent anywhere.
  • Foreground service — declared by Google Play’s asset delivery, which unpacks the game’s graphics after an install or update. No data is read or transmitted.

The app requests no access to your contacts, location, camera, microphone or photos. It stores its saves in its private storage and does not request broad access to your files; it writes a feedback archive to Downloads only when you ask for one.

Google Play

The distribution channel is not ours. When you download, install or update the app through Google Play, Google processes data about that — and, if you have enabled it on your device, may share crash diagnostics with developers in aggregated form. This processing is carried out by Google under its own terms, not by us. See Google’s Privacy Policy and the data safety section on the app’s Play Store page.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right of access (Art. 15), rectification (Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction (Art. 18), data portability (Art. 20) and objection (Art. 21), and the right to withdraw a consent at any time with effect for the future (Art. 7(3)).

In practice:

  • Statistics: unticking them in Settings withdraws your consent; the app then asks Unity to delete what it received from this installation. Deleting the cloud account does the same for this device. Statistics another device of yours sent are erased when you untick them there, or on request by e-mail with your Player ID if you used cloud save on that device. If you no longer have the app and never used cloud save, your statistics are known only under a random installation identifier we cannot connect to you, so we cannot find them (Art. 11 GDPR); their raw events expire after 13 months.
  • Cloud copies and account: delete your cloud account in the app under Settings → Gameplay → Account, or follow the steps on Delete your account, which a request by e-mail with your Player ID can do too.
  • Feedback reports: to get a copy of a report, or have it deleted, write to the contact address above. You may object to our use of a report at any time, and we will then delete it.

We answer requests without undue delay, normally within one month; if a request needs longer, we tell you why within that month. Never send passwords or sign-in codes.

You may complain to a data protection supervisory authority, in particular the one where you live or work. The authority for our location is the Landesbeauftragter für den Datenschutz und die Informationsfreiheit Rheinland-Pfalz.

Children

On Google Play the game is offered to players aged 13 and over. In Germany, a player can consent alone to a service like the gameplay statistics only from the age of 16 (Art. 8 GDPR). The app therefore asks whether you are 16 or older; below 16 the statistics stay off. Only a player who later answers in Settings that they are now 16 or older can tick them. Cloud save remains available. The app asks no birth date. A parent or guardian can untick both choices, delete the cloud account in the app, or write to the contact address above, and we will delete what we hold.
