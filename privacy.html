Privacy Policy

Version 1.13 · Last updated 14 November 2026

This policy explains what happens to your data when you play Conjunction (the “app”), an Android game distributed through Google Play, and when you visit this website. It covers Conjunction 0.6.2 and later, which offer optional online features: cloud save and gameplay statistics, and from Conjunction 0.6.3 the weekly arena. Earlier versions offer none of them and send none of the data described under Cloud save, Usage statistics and The arena. What changed since version 1.2 is listed under Changes to this policy.

The short version

Your saves stay on your device. Three features can send data, each only if you choose it, and the game plays the same without any of them: a backup of your heroes in the cloud; gameplay statistics, for which we ask your consent and which are only for players aged 16 or older; and the weekly arena, which you join at the Arena Master Z’tirom with its own consent, also only from 16, and where other players see the arena name of each hero you hand in. The two boxes for cloud save and statistics start empty, and you can change either at any time under Settings → Gameplay → Privacy; you leave the arena at Z’tirom. There is no advertising and no tracking across other apps. A feedback report reaches us only when you send it yourself.

What the app keeps on your device

The app keeps these on your device, in its private storage, and sends none of them unless you choose one of the features below or send a feedback report:

  • Your saves — each hero’s character, name as you chose it, progress, inventory, claimed fields and settings, so you can go on playing. They are needed to provide the game you play.
  • Your privacy choices — the two boxes, the version of the notice you answered, whether you said you are 16 or older, and whether you joined the arena and on which version of its terms, so the app can respect your choices and does not ask again. No birth date is asked or kept.
  • Fight recordings — the last fights, including any note you add with “Felt wrong?”, so a fight can be traced if you report a problem. They leave the device only inside a feedback report you send.
  • Error notes — when the app runs into an error, one line with the time, the app version, the kind of error, its message and the place in the app’s code where it happened, each kind once per app session; the newest 200 lines are kept. A line holds no identifier, no name and nothing you typed. The notes are kept whether or not you share statistics, so that a problem you report can be traced, and they leave the device only inside a feedback report you send — and, only while you share gameplay statistics, as an error event.
  • The play diary — only while you share gameplay statistics: one line for each moment of play (the app was started or brought back, a fight began or ended, a field was cleared for the first time, the hero died, a field’s entry check turned the hero away, the hero switched equipment set, a hunt was collected, a level was reached). A line holds game values such as the field, the hero’s level, the outcome, how long it took and what was found, a random identifier for the hero and one for the current app session, the app and game-data version, whether the game’s test options were used, and the time. While you also use cloud save, a line holds your cloud Player ID as well, so the statistics of your phones can be linked. It holds no name and nothing you typed. Unticking the statistics deletes the diary of every hero on the device.

Removing the app normally removes its private storage. A feedback archive you saved to Downloads, and any copy in a device backup, follow the settings of that storage. Neither removing the app nor unticking a box deletes what was already sent; see Your rights.

Before anything is sent

The first time you start the app — or, if you played an earlier version, the next time you open the world map — it shows a notice in two steps. The first names who is responsible and both features and asks once whether you are 16 or older. The second names in short what each feature sends and to whom, with a box for each, both empty, and points to this policy for the details. Below 16, the statistics box is not offered here; cloud save stays available. If you turn 16 later, you can correct your answer in Settings and then decide on the statistics. If you continue without ticking, you play without either feature.

Before you choose an online feature, your saves, settings and privacy choices remain on your device. The app does not start the statistics service or keep the play diary before you agree, and it does not create a cloud account before you tick cloud save. Cloud save uses only the identifiers and session data needed for the backup you asked for. Unticking the statistics stops new statistics and removes the diary; the app may still contact Unity once to ask for the statistics to be erased, and cloud save goes on if you ticked it. When you tick a box later in Settings, the app first shows what that feature sends and asks you to confirm.

Cloud save

Cloud save backs up each hero’s whole save — the hero’s name as you chose it, level, equipment, bag, gold, story progress and the hero’s random identifier — to Unity Gaming Services Cloud Save. The copy is sent about twenty seconds after the game saves, and only while cloud save is ticked; unticking it stops new copies, although a copy already on its way may still arrive. Before unticking takes effect, the app asks you and says what happens to the cloud account. An account no sign-in is linked to is then deleted, with every hero’s copy and backup kept under it, so nothing is left at Unity that you could no longer reach; an account you linked to Google Play Games is kept, the app only signs out of it, and Link with Google reaches it again, as on another device. Either way the session and the Player ID are removed from the device, the heroes on the device stay, and ticking cloud save again starts a new anonymous account. If the cloud cannot be reached when you untick, the app keeps the session and finishes this at the next start with a network. If the sign-in fails right after you tick cloud save, the app takes the tick back and only signs out, without a question and without deleting anything: an account no sign-in is linked to is then out of the app’s reach, as after an uninstall, and its copies stay in the cloud until you ask us to delete them with the Player ID (see Your rights).

Ticking cloud save creates an anonymous cloud account for this installation at Unity Gaming Services Authentication, with a random Player ID, and signs in to it; it asks for no Google sign-in and no other login. The app keeps a session on your device so it can reconnect at later starts without asking you. The device stores the Player ID and the session data, and Settings shows your Player ID. Unity Gaming Services also gives the app a random installation identifier, stored on your device and sent with the cloud save's requests. Until you link a sign-in, only this installation reaches the account: once you uninstall the app or clear its data, the app cannot reach it any more, and its copies stay in the cloud until you ask us to delete them with the Player ID (see Your rights); unticking cloud save deletes it, as described above.

To bring your heroes to another device, you can link Google Play Games to the cloud account under Settings → Gameplay → Account → Link with Google. The app starts the Google Play Games services only then — without that tap it does not contact Google. Google authenticates you — by itself if you are already signed in to Google Play Games on the device, otherwise in Google’s own sign-in window, which needs a Google Play Games profile — and gives the app a one-time code, which Unity Gaming Services Authentication exchanges for your Google Play Games account identifier and keeps with your cloud account. If you cancel or Google declines, nothing is linked and the backup goes on as before. Google Ireland Limited processes your sign-in under its own privacy policy, as an independent controller.

On another device, tick cloud save and tap Link with Google with the same Google account. Because that Google account already belongs to your cloud account, the app deletes the new device’s own anonymous account with its copies, signs in to your linked account and brings your heroes into the device’s free hero slots; the heroes already on the new device go up into that account. An account that has a linked sign-in is never deleted for this move. A cloud account an earlier 0.6.2 build linked to Google stays linked.

When the same hero changed on two devices, the app keeps the copy with more progress (level, then experience, then playing time; the newer save if they are equal) and stores the other copy as a backup in the same cloud account, one per hero, replaced by the next such backup. Settings names a backup it finds; we restore it on request. Deleting a hero on the device while cloud save is on also deletes its cloud copy and backup; deleting it while cloud save is off deletes them the next time cloud save is on, or with the cloud account. A hero deleted on one device is not brought back by another device’s copy that was not played since; a copy played there since is backed up again.

If the cloud account is deleted on another device or at your request, the app switches cloud save off on this device and tells you once; it does not create a new account by itself.

Legal basis: providing the backup you request by ticking cloud save, and the Google link you request by tapping Link with Google, Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR; storing the Player ID and session on your device is strictly necessary for that service (§ 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG). The copies, the backups, the account and the Google link are kept until you delete the hero or the cloud account, in the app under Settings → Gameplay → Account or by writing to us, or — for an account no sign-in is linked to — until you untick cloud save and confirm the question. Unticking it does not delete an account you linked to Google Play Games, and a sign-in that failed deletes nothing: such an account and its copies are kept until you ask us to delete them.

Usage statistics

If you are 16 or older and agree, the app sends the lines of the play diary to Unity Gaming Services Analytics so that we can see where the game is too hard or unclear and balance it. The game defines nine moments of play: app started or brought back, fight started, fight ended, field cleared for the first time, hero died, entry check failed, equipment set switched, hunt collected and level reached. Unity Analytics also records its own standard events, such as when a play session starts and ends.

With the statistics, the app also sends an error event when it runs into an error, so that we can find and fix faults: the kind of error, the first 120 characters of its message and the place in the app’s code (the same as in the error notes), once per kind and app session, with the identifiers of the diary — the hero’s only while a hero is played.

Analytics gives the app a random installation identifier, stored on your device; while cloud save is ticked and signed in, your Player ID is attached as well, so the statistics of a cloud player can be linked to that Player ID. Unticking cloud save signs out of the account or deletes it, and from then on the Player ID is no longer attached. With the random hero and session identifiers in the diary, this makes the statistics pseudonymous: we do not use them to identify you, do not combine them with other data and do not use them for advertising. Analytics also processes your IP address, the approximate region derived from it, and technical diagnostics and performance data of the app. The diary holds no name; a hero’s name reaches Unity only inside a cloud save copy.

Unity documents that it processes Analytics data on servers in Belgium and the Netherlands and keeps a replicated copy in Iowa, United States. Raw events are kept for 13 months; the counts and player profiles derived from them are kept until deletion is requested.

Legal basis: your consent, given by ticking I agree to gameplay statistics, Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, which also covers the diary, the identifiers and the events the app keeps on your device for this purpose (§ 25(1) TDDDG). You can withdraw your consent at any time by unticking the statistics under Settings → Gameplay → Privacy; this is as easy as giving it and does not affect the lawfulness of the processing before you withdrew. From then on the app collects no new statistics, deletes the diary on your device, and asks Unity to delete what it received from this installation — if your device is offline, at the next start that reaches Unity. Events the Analytics service had already queued on the device before you unticked may still arrive; the erasure covers them too. Unity deletes the data within seven days of receiving the request. If you tick the statistics again before Unity has confirmed an earlier erasure, the app keeps the new events on your device until it has.

The arena

From Conjunction 0.6.3 the Arena Master Z’tirom holds a tournament every week: the heroes players hand in fight each other, and a table shows how each did. Joining is a choice of its own. Before your first hand-in Z’tirom shows what is sent and who sees it, and asks you to confirm that you are 16 or older and that you join on these terms; below 16 the arena is not open to you. The arena needs cloud save, because a hand-in is stored in your cloud account. Neither cloud save nor the gameplay statistics join you to the arena, and the arena does not switch either of them on. The game plays the same without it.

What a hand-in sends. You can hand in up to three heroes a week, one per hero slot. For each, the app writes to Unity Gaming Services Cloud Save, under your cloud Player ID, a snapshot of the hero as it is at that moment: its random identifier, level, attributes, worn items with their rolls, active skills, skill-wheel progress, permanent treasure bonuses, the field it stands on (which decides the act it fights in), a fingerprint of its fight setup, which of the game’s test options were used, and the app and game-data version. The snapshot also carries the hero’s play history, which the tournament uses to check that the hero is one the game could have made: the fields it cleared, its farming runs (field, tier, experience and the items found, counted by rarity), its experience, its game clock, how often it was defeated, the legendaries it found and the items it bought or upgraded at the smith, counted by rarity. Only our tournament program reads this history; it is never shown to other players or published, and it is deleted with the hand-in, as described under “How long it is kept”. With the snapshot go the hero’s arena name, the week, the hero slot, a random identifier for the hand-in and whether it comes from a test build. Each hero you hand in carries its own arena name: the app fills in the name you gave the hero, you may keep it or change it, and you confirm it as public before the hand-in is sent; if you keep it, the hero’s name is that arena name. Apart from that, the snapshot does not hold the name you gave the hero, nor its bag, gold or story progress, the play diary, or anything about your device. If you leave the arena, the app writes one more note to Cloud Save that names the heroes you took back.

How the tournament runs. Once a week, after the deadline on Sunday at 18:00 Berlin time, we read the week’s hand-ins from Cloud Save onto our own computer in Germany. There we check that each hero is one the game can build, fight every hero against every other, and write the results back to Cloud Save: the table for every player, and for each of your heroes its own results — for each opponent the score and a short account of one fight — which only you can read. Your device never computes or sends a result. The best eight heroes of the table then fight a final. On the game’s internal test builds Z’tirom also holds test tournaments for our testers. They work the same way under keys of their own, are never posted on Discord or put on the season list, and only the latest test is kept.

What other players see. The table at Z’tirom shows each handed-in hero by its arena name, with its level, weapon, level band, place and results, so up to three arena names of yours can be public, one for each hero you hand in. The weekly winners — the first three of the final — are named by arena name, level and weapon on the season list at Z’tirom and in a post on the Conjunction Discord server, which everyone who joins that server can read. In your own results each opponent appears by the arena name the table shows for that hero, with its level, as “Hollin, level 23”, and so does your hero in theirs; the account of a fight names no one. Your Player ID, your snapshots and your results are never public. Choose arena names that are not your real name and offend no one: the game refuses some names, and we rename or remove a name that breaks this rule. You can report a name you find in the table: tap the hero at Z’tirom and choose Report, or write to the contact address above with the week and the name. A report made in the game is stored in your Cloud Save under your Player ID: the week, the row of the table and the arena name you reported. Our computer reads it, notes which name you reported in which week, so that a name counts once per player and week, and shows us the name to check. The reported player is not told about the report and never learns who made it.

Season leaderboards. Each season has leaderboards, one for each act of the game, that rank heroes by the in-game time at which they completed the act. Every test phase of the game is a season of its own, and a season ends when the next one begins, which may not be announced in advance. When a hero of the season that runs completes an act while you keep your heroes in the cloud and have joined the arena, the app writes a short report for that hero’s slot to Cloud Save, under your Player ID: the hero’s random identifier, the season, and for each leaderboard the hero has reached the in-game time, the moment it was reached and the app version, beside the season the report is for. A completion time is written once and stays the same when the act is played again. Once a week our computer reads the reports and checks each time against the hero’s cloud save copy — its milestones, the fields it cleared and its game clock — and leaves out a time the hero cannot have made, noting why. The leaderboards at Z’tirom show each hero once, by the name you gave the hero, with its level and its in-game time, to every player. Heroes from an earlier season or test phase stay playable but appear on no leaderboard. Your Player ID is never shown.

How long it is kept. A hand-in is deleted from Cloud Save as soon as its week has been fought. A week’s table and results stay until the next week has been fought, so about one week, and are then deleted. The season list of weekly winners stays until the season ends with the next game version for testers, and the winners of the season are then named once more in a Discord post. On our computer we keep the season’s hand-ins and the link from a table row to the player it belongs to until the season ends, so that a result can be corrected or erased; our computer also keeps, for each Discord winners post we keep, the identifier of the post and the Player IDs of the players it names, so that a post can be deleted when one of them asks. A Discord post stays on the server until we delete it, which we do when you ask. A report you made in the game stays in your Cloud Save until the week after its table has been fought, and our computer keeps who reported which name for the table that shows and the one before it, so about two weeks; both are then deleted. A season’s leaderboards stay while the season runs; when it ends they are kept, no longer changed, as that season’s archive. Your season reports stay in your Cloud Save as long as the hero’s slot holds them, and our computer keeps each ranked hero with the Player ID it belongs to for the season and its archive, so that a place can be erased.

Leaving and erasure. Leave the arena at Z’tirom withdraws your consent and every hero you handed in: they are not fought again, they leave the current table, and their results are deleted at the tournament’s next run, within seven days. It also deletes your season reports, and the next weekly run takes your heroes off the season’s leaderboards. Places you won in earlier weeks stay on the season list and in the Discord posts of those weeks until the season ends, and places in an archived season’s leaderboards stay in its archive; write to us with your Player ID, which Settings shows, and we erase them too, together with every copy on our computer. We may ask you to confirm that the Player ID is yours, without ever needing your password. Deleting your cloud account removes your hand-ins with it, and the tournament’s next run takes your heroes out of the table and deletes their results. Deleting the app alone deletes nothing that was already handed in.

Legal basis: your consent, given when you join the arena at Z’tirom, Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR. You can withdraw it at any time with Leave the arena; this does not affect the lawfulness of the processing before you withdrew. Keeping your choice on the device is part of the arena you asked for (§ 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG). A report of a name is part of the arena you joined; we also process it in our legitimate interest in keeping the arena’s names free of insults and impersonation (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Feedback reports

The game has a feedback button for reporting bugs and ideas. When you tap it and send, the app packs one file containing:

  • the text you typed and whether it is a bug or an idea;
  • the app version, your device model and its Android version;
  • the save game of every hero on this device — each character and the name you gave it, progress, inventory, skills and settings, and whether the game’s test options were used;
  • the play diary of every hero on this device, if you share gameplay statistics;
  • the error notes of this device;
  • the fight recordings not sent in an earlier report, including any note you added to a fight with “Felt wrong?”.

The app does not send this file itself. It saves the file in your device’s Downloads folder and opens Android’s share menu. You choose the app it goes through (for example your e-mail app or Discord) and the recipient; for e-mail the address feedback@conjunctiongame.com is filled in for you. If you cancel, nothing is sent. The copy in Downloads stays until your next report replaces it or you delete it.

We use what you send only to find and fix errors and to balance the game. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in doing that, Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR. We keep a report for as long as we are working on what it describes, and delete it earlier if you ask. The app you send it through also handles the report, along with whatever it carries with it, such as your e-mail address or Discord name, under its own terms. If you post a report in a channel that other people can read, they can see it too.

Permissions the app requests

  • Network access — for the cloud save and the gameplay statistics while you have ticked them, for the Google sign-in you link to cloud save, to ask Unity to erase statistics after you untick them, and for the arena’s hand-ins, its table and the names you report once you have joined it.
  • Vibration — for haptic feedback on critical hits. No data is read or transmitted.
  • Notifications — so the game can tell you when a field you set to farm has finished. These notifications are generated on your device by the app itself; no server is involved and no information is sent anywhere.
  • Foreground service — declared by Google Play’s asset delivery, which unpacks the game’s graphics after an install or update. No data is read or transmitted.

The app requests no access to your contacts, location, camera, microphone or photos. It stores its saves in its private storage and does not request broad access to your files; it writes a feedback archive to Downloads only when you ask for one.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right of access (Art. 15), rectification (Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction (Art. 18), data portability (Art. 20) and objection (Art. 21), and the right to withdraw a consent at any time with effect for the future (Art. 7(3)).

In practice:

  • Statistics: unticking them in Settings withdraws your consent; the app then asks Unity to delete what it received from this installation. Deleting the cloud account does the same for this device. Statistics another device of yours sent are erased when you untick them there, or on request by e-mail with your Player ID if you used cloud save on that device. If you no longer have the app and never used cloud save, your statistics are known only under a random installation identifier we cannot connect to you, so we cannot find them (Art. 11 GDPR); their raw events expire after 13 months.
  • Cloud copies and account: delete your cloud account in the app under Settings → Gameplay → Account, or follow the steps on Delete your account, which a request by e-mail with your Player ID can do too.
  • Arena: Leave the arena at Z’tirom withdraws your consent and your heroes. To have everything of yours erased — also your places on the season list, the Discord posts that name you, the reports you made and our copies — write to the contact address above with your Player ID. To report another player’s arena name, tap the hero in the table at Z’tirom, or write to the same address.
  • Feedback reports: to get a copy of a report, or have it deleted, write to the contact address above. You may object to our use of a report at any time, and we will then delete it.

We answer requests without undue delay, normally within one month; if a request needs longer, we tell you why within that month. Never send passwords or sign-in codes.

You may complain to a data protection supervisory authority, in particular the one where you live or work. The authority for our location is the Landesbeauftragter für den Datenschutz und die Informationsfreiheit Rheinland-Pfalz.

Children

On Google Play the game is offered to players aged 13 and over. In Germany, a player can consent alone to a service like the gameplay statistics only from the age of 16 (Art. 8 GDPR). The app therefore asks whether you are 16 or older; below 16 the statistics stay off. Only a player who later answers in Settings that they are now 16 or older can tick them. Cloud save remains available. The arena is also only for players aged 16 or older; Z’tirom asks before the first hand-in, and below 16 no hero can be handed in. A player who joined and later says they are under 16 has every hero they handed in withdrawn, as Leave the arena does. The app asks no birth date. A parent or guardian can untick both choices, delete the cloud account in the app, or write to the contact address above, and we will delete what we hold.
